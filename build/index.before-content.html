Strategies Acts of kindness Managing stress Gratitude
Ferguson et al. (2012) · Transfusion

Blood donors are driven by a “warm glow”, the good feeling of giving itself, more than by pure altruism.

Thoits & Hewitt (2001) · J. Health & Soc. Behavior

Volunteering improved all six well-being measures in 2,681 adults, and happier people went on to volunteer more.

Our link: giving first turned our nerves into calm, and that calm carried into every donor after. Kindness and stress management in one morning.

This is acts of kindness, gratitude and managing stress. Ferguson found donors are driven by a warm glow, the good feeling of giving itself. And Thoits and Hewitt showed volunteering improves well-being, and happier people volunteer more.

Linked to sustainable-happiness research

Fully present, eight hours straight.

Strategies Living in the present Body & soul Acts of kindness
Killingsworth & Gilbert (2010) · Science

Our minds wander ~47% of the time, and we're less happy when they do. Handing water to runner after runner left no room to wander.

Lyubomirsky, Sheldon & Schkade (2005) · Rev. General Psychology

Five kind acts in a single day lifted happiness; spread across a week, they didn't. Our back-to-back weekend was exactly that burst.

This is living in the present and caring for body and soul. Killingsworth and Gilbert found our minds wander almost half the time, and we're less happy when they do; handing out water left no room for that. And Lyubomirsky found five kind acts in one day beat spreading them out, like our back-to-back weekend.
Contradictions · re-examined positions

Class vs. reality

Position re-examined
In class

Helping only lifts well-being when it's freely chosen, not done for a reward.

Weinstein & Ryan (2010)
We found

We started for a grade, yet it felt real. Somewhere between the forms and the medals, our motivation became our own.

In class

About 40% of happiness is under our intentional control, the "happiness pie".

Lyubomirsky et al. (2005)
We found

We could only help because we were healthy enough to donate, free that weekend, and in a city that makes volunteering easy. Circumstances matter more than the pie suggests.

cf. Brown & Rohrer (2020), J. Happiness Studies
In class

An act of kindness means a giver helps a receiver.

We re-examined

At langar we served, and then the same people served us. Kindness felt less like charity and more like belonging.

We also found contradictions. Weinstein and Ryan show helping lifts well-being only when it's freely chosen, and honestly, we started for a grade. Somewhere between the forms and the medals, that stopped mattering. The 40 percent "happiness pie" also felt too neat: we could help only because we were healthy, free, and in a city that makes volunteering easy. And at langar, kindness stopped feeling like charity. No giver, no receiver, just belonging.
