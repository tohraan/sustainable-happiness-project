Acts of kindness
Warm glow
Ferguson et al. (2012) · Transfusion

In a study of blood donors, the good feeling of giving (the warm glow) explained why people donate better than pure concern for others did.

UsWe donated, then spent two hours helping others donate. The glow lasted the whole session, not just our minutes in the chair.

Managing stress
Stress buffer
Raposa, Laws & Ansell (2016) · Clin. Psych. Science

In a 14-day diary study, on days people helped others more, everyday stress hit their mood less.

UsGuiding nervous donors gave our own pre-needle nerves somewhere to go. Helping turned waiting into calm.

Acts of kindness: Ferguson found blood donors give for the warm glow of giving, more than pure altruism; we felt it all session. Managing stress: Raposa found that on days people help more, stress hurts their mood less. Guiding nervous donors calmed our own nerves.

Dunbar (2017) · Adaptive Human Behavior & Physiology

People who eat with others more often are happier, trust others more and feel closer to their community.

Gratitude lifts outlook

People who listed what they were grateful for each week felt better about their lives and more optimistic.

UsSeva is gratitude acted out: thanks given as a free meal, and we joined in.

kindness

Belonging

We also found contradictions. Weinstein and Ryan show helping lifts well-being most when it's freely chosen, and honestly, we started for a grade; by the medals, the motivation was our own. The forty percent happiness pie felt too neat: we could help only because we were healthy, free and in a city that makes it easy. And at langar, the people we served then served us. Kindness felt less like charity, more like belonging.
What we're taking away

Sustainable happiness
isn't found.
It's served.

Happiness that contributes to individual, community or global well-being without exploiting other people, the environment or future generations. (O'Brien, 2010)
