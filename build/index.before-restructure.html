Langar Seva

Guru Nanak Darbar Gurudwara, Jebel Ali, the UAE's first official gurudwara (opened 2012). Its langar is a free kitchen open to every faith. Seva means selfless service.

“Pehle pangat, phir sangat.”
First eat together as equals, then pray together. (Guru Amar Das)
At Guru Nanak Darbar Gurudwara in Jebel Ali, the UAE's first official gurudwara, the langar kitchen feeds anyone of any faith for free. We helped organise the dal, roti, chawal and meethe chawal and served it to everyone. Then we sat in pangat and were served ourselves. That circle is the whole idea of seva.

Blood camp

Research says

Voluntary, unpaid donors are the safest blood supply, and they gave over 85% of the world's ~120 million donations.

WHO, global blood safety data (2023)
Langar seva
A free meal with dignity

No one is asked who they are. Everyone sits together and eats the same food.

Research says

People who eat with others report higher happiness and life satisfaction than those who always eat alone (UK panel of ~2,000 adults).

Dunbar (2017), Breaking Bread, Adaptive Human Behavior & Physiology
Unity Run
Every ability, one finish line

People of Determination ran free with a companion, alongside every age group.

Research says

Across 515 studies in 38 countries, contact between groups reliably reduces prejudice, and an inclusive run is exactly that contact.

Pettigrew & Tropp (2006), J. Personality & Social Psychology
What did it give the community? One donation can help up to three patients, and voluntary donors give over 85 percent of the world's blood. Langar gives a free meal with dignity, and Dunbar found that people who eat together are happier and more satisfied with life. A review of 515 studies shows contact between groups reduces prejudice, and an inclusive run is exactly that. Over to Vedansh.

Alignment to the happiness strategies

Every stop, many strategies

Now the happiness strategies. Every activity was an act of kindness, and doing them together nurtured our relationships. Seva is gratitude in practice. The run and the langar were care for body and soul, and handing water to runners keeps you fully in the present. Donating blood also meant managing our own nerves first.
Additional research on the strategies

Why it made us happier

Blood camp · acts of kindness

Blood donors are moved by a “warm glow”, the good feeling of giving itself. Kindness pays the giver back.

Ferguson et al. (2012), Transfusion
Langar · social relationships

The happiest 10% of people studied all had one thing in common: strong social relationships. Langar is built on sitting together.

Diener & Seligman (2002), Psychological Science
Unity Run · living in the present

Minds wander ~47% of the time, and people are less happy when they do. Handing water to runner after runner left no room to wander.

Killingsworth & Gilbert (2010), Science
The thread tying all three together

Doing 5 kind acts in one day lifted happiness. Spreading them over a week didn't. Our blood-camp day and back-to-back weekend were exactly those bursts.

Lyubomirsky, Sheldon & Schkade (2005)

Volunteering improved all six well-being measures in a panel of 2,681 adults, and happier people went on to volunteer more. It's a loop.

Thoits & Hewitt (2001)

Spending on others rather than ourselves made people happier. We gave blood, food and time instead of money.

Dunn, Aknin & Norton (2008), Science

The research backs each stop. Blood donors feel a "warm glow" from giving itself. The happiest people have strong social relationships, which is what langar is built on. Our minds wander almost half the time, which makes us less happy; handing out water left no room for that. And Lyubomirsky found five kind acts in one day lift happiness more than spreading them out, like our back-to-back weekend. Thoits and Hewitt showed volunteering improves well-being, and happier people volunteer more.
Contradictions · re-examined positions

Class vs. reality

Position re-examined
In class

Helping only lifts well-being when it's freely chosen, not done for a reward.

Weinstein & Ryan (2010)
We found

We started for a grade, yet it felt real. Somewhere between the forms and the medals, our motivation became our own.

In class

About 40% of happiness is under our intentional control, the "happiness pie".

Lyubomirsky et al. (2005)
We found

We could only help because we were healthy enough to donate, free that weekend, and in a city that makes volunteering easy. Circumstances matter more than the pie suggests.

cf. Brown & Rohrer (2020), J. Happiness Studies
In class

An act of kindness means a giver helps a receiver.

We re-examined

At langar we served, and then the same people served us. Kindness felt less like charity and more like belonging.

We also found contradictions. Weinstein and Ryan show helping lifts well-being only when it's freely chosen, and honestly, we started for a grade. Somewhere between the forms and the medals, that stopped mattering. The 40 percent "happiness pie" also felt too neat: we could help only because we were healthy, free, and in a city that makes volunteering easy. And at langar, kindness stopped feeling like charity. No giver, no receiver, just belonging.
